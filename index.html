Memories

These park-bench photographs mark an important moment in my friendship and collaboration with Ali Dehghannejad, known as Alivexor. Ali is from Tehran, and today I consider him my best friend and collaborator. I was in tenth grade, around fifteen or sixteen, and Ali was around fifteen and one school year below me. For THE APEX, our Minecraft story-server project, my mother, who is a lawyer, helped prepare a collaboration agreement. I travelled to Tehran so Ali and his father could sign it; we were teenagers and involved our families in the process. My uncle Siamak Nournezhad accompanied me. He was also taking my grandmother to see a doctor, and I joined the trip. That day I met Ali’s father and his two younger brothers. We sat in a park and had banana milk. I had dressed up, and my uncle had styled my hair. These are more than just photographs on a bench to me: they are memories of an important friend and the work we wanted to build together.

personal-033

Image

Book material

These photographs show the book-authorship appreciation certificate and the flowers from a day that meant a great deal to me. I had given the education department a copy of my book months earlier. When the authors’ recognition event finally came, I was told I was the only fifteen-year-old in the group and that perhaps I should not attend among the adults. I felt that having a young author should have been a reason to welcome me, and I insisted on going. We went from school to the education department. I remember feeling overlooked on arrival and having to explain that I was an author. After roughly an hour of names being called, mine was called last. By then, as I remember it, some people had left and others were looking at their phones. I felt that the moment had passed without the attention I had hoped for. This is my personal memory of the day, rather than a claim about anyone’s intentions. I remember receiving 500,000 tomans with the certificate. My book was priced at 350,000 tomans, and I had already given them a copy as a gift. In my disappointment I compared those figures: 150,000 tomans above the price of the book I had donated. To me, the recognition felt small compared with the work and anticipation behind it. The bouquet made me happy. My uncle Siamak, who was there, bought it for me. My mother and Akhtar Amir Sadeghi—whom I call “Aziz”—were also present. My father could not come. My mother took these photographs that night. Alongside the disappointment, I remember the warmth of my family and the flowers. Date note: I recall the event as late Dey 1404, close to Bahman. The photographed certificate itself is dated 1404/09/09; its printed date and my recollection of the ceremony date are kept separate pending clarification.
